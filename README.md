Root water uptake (RWU) is a key process linking subsurface moisture redistribution and the atmospheric water cycle. However, it is difficult to quantitatively simulate under strong heterogeneity of the medium or a lack of hydraulic parameters, especially for weathered bedrock. Therefore, we developed a non‑parametric, observation‑driven diagnostic framework that integrates mass conservation with water‑demand allocation to infer layer‑wise flux and RWU across soil and weathered bedrock profiles. We further applied it in two larch plantation plots underlain by sandstone and tuff. It clearly reversed the layer-wise moisture dynamics driven by freeze-thaw cycles, infiltration, and transpiration. Then, the RWU sourced from different layers were estimated. Although soil moisture supported the dominant water source for the shallow-rooted larch plantation (over 90%), RWU from the weathered bedrock layer still cannot be ignored. The weathered sandstone moisture provided about 7.85% and 3.25% RWU (based on lower-bound estimations) during the growing and post-growing seasons, respectively, whereas it was about 1.13% and 4.88% from weathered tuff. Additionally, this framework can also estimate the layer-wise vertical drainage flux. It was verified that the weathered bedrock layers act as deep moisture buffers during the wet season and provide stable water retention during the dry season. Our study provides a reproducible computational framework for simulating moisture transport and RWU in profiles where parameters are difficult to obtain or where the medium heterogeneity is strong. Our results also highlighted that incorporating bedrock moisture dynamics into evapotranspiration frameworks is essential for improving assessments of drought resilience and guiding water‑limited afforestation strategies.
